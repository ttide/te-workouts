Workout guide

ChestBackShouldersLegsArmsCore

New to training, or back after a long break (under 3 months of regular training).

2–3 sets. Finish each set with about 3 reps still in the tank. Rest 60–90 seconds. Learn the movement before adding weight.

Your week: 3 days a week, with a rest day between. Day A: Chest, Back, Core. Day B: Legs, Shoulders, Arms. Alternate A and B.

3–12 months of regular training, comfortable with the basic lifts.

3 sets. Finish each set with 1–2 reps in the tank. Rest 60–120 seconds.

Your week: 4 days a week. Day 1: Chest and Shoulders. Day 2: Back and Arms. Day 3: Legs and Core. Day 4: your weakest two parts.

More than a year of regular training, with good form on squats, presses and rows.

3–4 sets. Heavy lifts first with 2–3 minutes' rest; the rest at 60–90 seconds. Take hard sets close to failure, never past good form.

Your week: 5 days a week. Day 1: Chest. Day 2: Back. Day 3: Legs. Day 4: Shoulders and Arms. Day 5: Legs and Core.

Chest

  1. Incline push-up2 × 8–12

    Hands on a bench; body in one straight line from head to heels.

  2. Chest press machine3 × 10–12

    Handles at mid-chest; press without locking the elbows hard.

  3. Flat dumbbell press2 × 10–12

    Lower until the dumbbells are beside your chest, then press up and slightly in.

  4. Cable chest fly2 × 12–15

    Soft bend in the elbows; bring your hands together in a wide hug.

  1. Flat dumbbell press3 × 8–12

    Shoulder blades pulled back and down into the bench.

  2. Incline dumbbell press3 × 8–12

    Bench at 30°; lower with control.

  3. Cable chest fly3 × 12–15

    Squeeze for a second where your hands meet.

  4. Push-up2 × As many as you can with 2 left

    Chest to fist height from the floor, elbows about 45° from your body.

  1. Bench press4 × 6–8

    Feet planted, bar to lower chest. Use a spotter or safety arms.

  2. Incline dumbbell press3 × 8–10

    Full range; pause briefly at the bottom.

  3. Parallel bar dip3 × 8–12

    Lean slightly forward; lower until shoulders are just below elbows if comfortable.

  4. Cable chest fly3 × 12–15

    Slow on the way back; feel the stretch.

  5. Push-up2 × Close to failure

    Finisher. Keep your body straight to the last rep.

Back

  1. Lat pulldown3 × 10–12

    Pull the bar to your upper chest; chest up, no swinging.

  2. Seated cable row3 × 10–12

    Sit tall; pull towards your lower ribs without leaning back.

  3. One-arm dumbbell row2 × 10–12 each side

    Hand and knee on the bench; pull the dumbbell to your hip.

  4. Face pull2 × 12–15

    Rope to forehead height; elbows high and wide.

  1. Lat pulldown3 × 8–12

    Lead with your elbows; control the bar back up.

  2. One-arm dumbbell row3 × 8–12 each side

    Keep your back flat and hips square.

  3. Seated cable row3 × 10–12

    Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the end.

  4. Back extension2 × 10–12

    Hinge at the hips; stop when your body is in a straight line.

  5. Face pull3 × 12–15

    Pause for a second with the rope at your face.

  1. Pull-up4 × 6–10

    Use a band or the assisted machine if needed. Chin over the bar, full hang at the bottom.

  2. Bent-over row4 × 6–8

    Hinge to about 45°; bar to lower ribs; keep your back flat.

  3. One-arm dumbbell row3 × 8–10 each side

    Heavy but strict; no twisting.

  4. Seated cable row3 × 10–12

    Full stretch forward, full squeeze back.

  5. Face pull3 × 15

    Light and strict; good for shoulder health.

  3. Reverse fly2 × 12–15

    Chest on an incline bench; open your arms wide.

  4. Face pull2 × 12–15

    Elbows high; pull the rope apart at your face.

  3. Reverse fly3 × 12–15

    Squeeze at the top; slow on the way down.

  5. Standing calf raise2 × 15

Before you start

5–10 minutes of easy cardio, then 1–2 light sets of your first exercise.

How to progress

When you can do the top of the rep range on every set with good form, add the smallest weight step next time. If you can't reach the bottom of the range, go lighter.

Safety. Stop if you feel sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain or unusual breathlessness, and tell a trainer. If you have an injury or medical condition, are pregnant, or are recovering from surgery, speak to a trainer and your doctor first. This is a general guide, not a personal programme. Personal-training members should follow their trainer's plan.
